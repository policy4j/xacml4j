**Techniques and what they add**

1. **Sound policy slicing with an "Any" bucket.** Source: Cutler et al., "Cedar: A New Language for Expressive, Fast, Safe, and Analyzable Authorization", OOPSLA 2024 (arXiv 2403.04651v2, §3.3, §5.3), peer-reviewed; soundness of slicing is among the Lean-proved properties (Torlak, FMiTF 2024 slides). Mechanism: every policy is keyed by the entity its scope names for principal and for resource, or `Any` when the scope names none; a request looks up the cross product of its principal, its resource, their ancestors, and `Any`; the slice is the union of those buckets, so an unconstrained policy is always evaluated. Reported gain: without slicing the template encodings were "much poorer"; with slicing, performance returned to that of the encoding without templates, median 18% faster on the GitHub-like model and 94% slower on the Drive-like one; Cedar's own medians are 4.0 to 5.0 µs and 11 µs per request, against Rego 76 to 676 µs on the same models (§5.2.2). Mapping: this is the shape the notes propose for `PolicySet.combineDecisions` (`PolicySet.java:177`) in front of `DenyOverrides.doCombine` (`DenyOverrides.java:73`, Deny returns at 77) and `FirstApplicable` (`FirstApplicable.java:52`); the delta is the key design: a two-attribute key (subject × resource, or role × action for rsa2008-shaped sets) built as a cross product of the request's bag values plus `Any`, instead of one leading designator, and the `Any` bucket as the only fallback set. Semantics: candidates stay in declaration order; the `Any` bucket holds every child whose target is not provably No-match by an indexed equality (§7.7 tables: a No-match `AnyOf` makes the target No-match; the engine's current error order, bugs policy4j-yeu3 and policy4j-0972, restricts eligibility to the first `AnyOf` and first `Match` positions until fixed); the key designator is resolved through the context once, so PIP values and §7.3.5 hold; `MustBePresent="true"` children go to `Any`; an Indeterminate key resolution evaluates the whole list; §7.18 staging is untouched because skipped children never reached `BaseCompositeDecisionRule.evaluate`. Effort M, risk M to H (eligibility classifier, repository invalidation for referenced children).

2. **Conjunction counting index for `AllOf` with several `Match` elements.** Source: Fabret, Jacobsen, Llirbat, Pereira, Ross, Shasha, "Filtering algorithms and implementation for very fast publish/subscribe systems", SIGMOD 2001, pp. 115 to 126, peer-reviewed (verified through two secondary citations; its figures were not fetched, UNVERIFIED). Mechanism: one inverted index per attribute key from literal to the list of (child, `AllOf`) entries; a request's values hit entries, a counter per `AllOf` reaches its number of indexed `Match` elements, the child becomes a candidate. It replaces PR6 H8's variable-ordered trie for multi-attribute targets with no ordering heuristic. Mapping: `MatchAllOf.match` (`MatchAllOf.java:68`) and `Match.match` (`Match.java:111`) define the entries; a non-equality `Match` (regexp, order, selector) makes its `AllOf` unindexable, which sends the child to the `Any` bucket. Semantics as in 1. Effort M (shares the classifier with 1), risk M.

3. **A matching tree at every level.** Source: Pina Ros, Lischka, Gómez Mármol, "Graph-based XACML evaluation", SACMAT 2012, pp. 83 to 92, peer-reviewed, 72 citations; a Matching Tree finds the applicable rules and a Combining Tree evaluates only them under the combining algorithms (abstract via Semantic Scholar and ResearchGate; the paper is paywalled, its numbers UNVERIFIED). Delta for xacml4j: the same index inside `Policy` over its rules (`Policy.combineDecisions`, `Policy.java:172`), which is where rsa2008 spends its time (PR6 H8: `Target.match` 11.8% of rsa2008 CPU; PR6 H6: multi-rule policies 66.6% inclusive). Semantics: rule order per §C; rule advice and obligations are staged in the policy's `StagedResponses` only for evaluated rules, which is unchanged because a skipped rule is No-match. Effort S on top of 1 and 2, risk M.

4. **Full compilation to decision diagrams.** Sources: Liu, Chen, Hwang, Xie, "XEngine", SIGMETRICS 2008, peer-reviewed: two orders of magnitude faster than Sun PDP on real-life policies with hundreds of rules, three to four orders on synthetic policies with thousands of rules, via numericalization, normalization to one first-applicable flat policy, and a decision tree; Ngo, Demchenko, de Laat, "Decision Diagrams for XACML Policy Evaluation and Management", Computers & Security 49, March 2015, peer-reviewed: the earlier engines "cannot handle errors containing indeterminate decisions, or ignore the critical attribute setting"; MIDD keeps them with interval partitions (figures paywalled, UNVERIFIED). Verdict for xacml4j: the pre-filter of 1 to 3 gets the asymptotic part of the gain and keeps §7.11 to §7.18 in the existing combining algorithms; a compiled diagram would have to reproduce Indeterminate{D,P,DP}, PIP timing, reference resolution, and staging. Effort L, risk H; not for this round.

5. **OPA rule indexing and early exit.** Source: openpolicyagent.org "Policy Performance" (current page, not peer-reviewed) and the blog "Optimizing OPA: Rule Indexing" (OPA 0.4.9). Rules that carry over directly: an `or` is indexed only when every operand is indexable, a negation never, a bare presence check is indexed, and "early exit is checked after the indexing lookup", which is the structure of 1 to 3 followed by the combining algorithm's own short-circuit. Comprehension indexing is a per-decision memo keyed by an outer variable, which xacml4j already has in `VariableDefinition.evaluate` (`VariableDefinition.java:115`) and the handler memos (`DefaultEvaluationContextHandler.java:63` to 88). Nothing beyond 1 to 3 to take.

6. **Partial evaluation and constant folding.** Sources: OPA "Partial Evaluation" blog (residual policies for later inputs, built on rule indexing), Cedar `partial-eval` and `tpe` experimental features (cedar-policy CHANGELOG, docs.rs), Prezta (arXiv 2607.11466v2, July 2026, preprint, not peer-reviewed: XACML compiled to Rust, 83% of the conformance suite; its gains are zkVM prover times). For xacml4j, folding literal-only `Apply` subtrees at build time (`Apply.java:255` to 270, `Condition.java:87`) would need function purity and error memoization; the rsa2008 corpus has no literal-only expression at all (function histogram: string-equal 7, any-of 7, string-one-and-only 5, not 5, string-subset 1, and 1), and no `*-bag` constructor. A per-PEP residual root policy is a product feature, not an evaluator change. Effort M, gain 0 on the current corpora; not now.

7. **Attribute retrieval caching across decisions.** Sources: AuthzForce core README and CHANGELOG (pluggable attribute providers, `beginMultipleDecisionRequest`; context-dependent decision cache), OPA "External Data" page (caching of `http.send` data is left to the remote service's headers); none peer-reviewed. xacml4j already has the SPI: `ResolverDescriptor.getPreferredCacheTTL()` (`ResolverDescriptor.java:95`), `BasePolicyInformationPointCacheProvider` TTL checks (lines 95 and 107), `ResolverCacheKey`, and the no-cache default (`PolicyInformationPoint.java:94`, `noCacheProvider` at 113). What is missing is a PIP-heavy corpus (D12 records none) and a default bounded in-memory provider. Semantics: §7.3.5 inside a decision is the cycle A memo; across decisions, staleness is bounded by the resolver's declared TTL, and the decision cache TTL already takes the minimum (D9). Effort S, risk L, gain unknown until measured.

8. **Decision cache keyed by request.** Source: AuthzForce DecisionCache extension (README; CHANGELOG adds an `EvaluationContext` parameter for context-dependent caches), not peer-reviewed. xacml4j: `PolicyDecisionCache` with the no-op default (`PolicyDecisionPointBuilder.java:69`), used at `DefaultPolicyDecisionPoint.java:138` to 166 with `getDecisionCacheTTL()`. An application choice, not an evaluator technique; no work.

Not worth a cycle: XEngine's numericalization (interning literals), because D7 already stores primitive values and `StringVal.equals` on short strings is not on any profile; adaptive reordering and clustering (Marouf, Shehab, Squicciarini, Sundareswaran, IEEE TSC 4(4), 2011, peer-reviewed), refuted in `synthesis.md` because evaluation order is observable through short-circuits and §7.18.
