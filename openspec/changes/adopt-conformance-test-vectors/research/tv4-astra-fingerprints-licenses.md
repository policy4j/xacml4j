**OASIS copyright and IPR findings**

The current [OASIS IPR Policy §14.1–14.3](https://www.oasis-open.org/policies-guidelines/ipr/) permits copying covered documents with notices preserved and permits specified explanatory or implementation-assisting derivatives. It also restricts modification of the document itself. Section 14.2 supplies a shorter notice for machine-readable deliverables. These terms are not an unrestricted software license.

The [legacy IPR policy §3.1](https://www.oasis-open.org/policies-guidelines/ipr-legacy/) grants contribution rights **to OASIS**. That is not, by itself, a downstream license to relicense every contribution. A [December 2006 XACML TC discussion](https://groups.oasis-open.org/communities/community-home/digestviewer/viewthread?MessageKey=8218fa75-46ad-4a96-8ec8-cffebf269674) confirms that XACML 2.0 was developed under the legacy policy and discusses missing notices in schemas. It does not specifically license the conformance package.

The [2017 XACML 3.0 specification’s Notices](https://docs.oasis-open.org/xacml/3.0/errata01/os/xacml-3.0-core-spec-errata01-os-complete.html) explicitly carry OASIS copyright and redistribution terms. I could not establish an equally explicit package-level notice for the legacy test XML. **Do not infer that fixtures are public domain, automatically Apache/MIT, or covered retroactively by the current policy.** Also, the OASIS wiki’s “GPL licensed” footer identifies its MoinMoin software; it does not establish a GPL license for linked test vectors.

Practical licensing conclusions:

- **Can vendor:** project-owned MIT/Apache-2.0 fixtures under their existing terms, preserving LICENSE, NOTICE and attribution. This includes AuthzForce’s own regression/JSON validation material. Repository declarations provide a redistribution basis for their contributions; they do not erase inherited rights.
- **Cannot adopt as LGPL-only:** GPL-covered RESTful-PDP material cannot simply receive an LGPL label. [GPLv3 §5](https://github.com/authzforce/restful-pdp/blob/develop/LICENSE) permits aggregation of separate independent works, so a separately licensed GPL test collection is not categorically forbidden in an LGPL repository. That requires keeping the GPL obligations and assessing the actual arrangement.
- **Unclear:** unique material from the unlicensed mirrors, the precise legacy OASIS package grant, and the unverified XACMLight fixture modifications. For identical files, use the better-documented upstream instead.

**Corrections and engine conventions must remain separate**

AuthzForce’s [README](https://github.com/authzforce/core/blob/develop/pdp-testutils/src/test/resources/conformance/xacml-3.0-from-2.0-ct/README.md) is useful evidence of differences, but not normative authority. It documents several distinct categories:

- Schema repairs, including obsolete attributes, invalid timezone values and XPath URI spelling.
- Harness choices, including rejecting invalid policies during initialization and renaming accompanying files to `.ignore`.
- Engine conventions: overriding supplied current-time values unless an issuer is provided; changing `IIIE302` response attribute order to match the engine; and a particular interpretation of applicable policies for `ReturnPolicyIdList`.
- Unsupported features, including some mixed-datatype attribute cases and multiple-root-policy cases.

Those categories need different dispositions. A fixture correction requires an OASIS justification; a harness choice requires an adapter; an engine convention must not silently become xacml4j’s conformance rule. The official [2.0 test description](https://www.oasis-open.org/committees/download.php/14877/ConformanceTests.html) itself calls the tests non-normative and requires semantic equivalence of responses.

**Recommended adoption and streamlining**

1. **Keep the current AT&T-derived corpus once.** Preserve its bytes and attach explicit source/version/license provenance. Do not count mirrors, renamed copies or deprecated files as independent coverage.
2. **Review AuthzForce’s 23 additional case directories first**, followed by its standard JSON request/response validation cases. Their semantic novelty relative to xacml4j’s other unit tests remains UNVERIFIED.
3. Extend the existing manifest approach with provenance and expected-phase information: policy rejection, request rejection, evaluation response, or profile-specific validation. The current [ConformanceManifest.java](xacml-conformance-tests/src/test/java/org/xacml4j/v20/ConformanceManifest.java:38) already records paths, disposition, reason and bug ID. The [runner](xacml-conformance-tests/src/test/java/org/xacml4j/v20/ConformanceCaseRunner.java:143) already handles static rejection and request syntax errors; its [NaN adjudication](xacml-conformance-tests/src/test/java/org/xacml4j/v20/ConformanceCaseRunner.java:190) demonstrates preserving fixture bytes while documenting a standards-based expectation.
4. Model resolver requirements explicitly. For example, the official [IIF002 instructions](https://www.oasis-open.org/apps/org/workgroup/xacml/download.php/14887/IIF002Special.txt) require versioned policy retrieval. Do not substitute another engine’s skip list or a decision-only comparator.
